      Sources & methodology

      Effect sizes are taken from large cohort studies and meta-analyses. Country baselines are life expectancy at birth from the WHO Global Health Observatory (latest available year per country). Where evidence is mixed (e.g. light-to-moderate alcohol), the conservative effect is used.

      • WHO Global Health Observatory — life expectancy at birth by country and sex (2021/2023).
      • GBD 2019 Risk Factors Collaborators (Lancet 2020) — population-attributable years of life lost for smoking, high BMI, high blood pressure, alcohol, air pollution, and other risk factors.
      • Doll et al., BMJ 2004 — 50-year follow-up of British doctors; smoking-attributable mortality.
      • Jha et al., NEJM 2013 — hazards of continuing vs quitting smoking by age.
      • Inoue-Choi et al., 2020 — mortality in "social" occasional smokers.
      • Arem et al., JAMA 2019 — dose-response of leisure-time physical activity and all-cause mortality.
      • Cappuccio et al., Sleep 2010 — short and long sleep duration and all-cause mortality.
      • Aune et al., Int J Epidemiol 2017 — fruit/vegetable intake and cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.
      • Russ et al., BMJ 2012 — psychological distress / self-reported stress and mortality.
      • Cutler & Lleras-Muney, 2006 — education and health outcomes.
      • Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010 — social relationships and all-cause mortality.
      • WHO Global status report on road safety — seatbelt, helmet, and motorcycle risk factors.
      • Liss et al., 2021 — annual physical exams and mortality outcomes.

      This is a teaching tool, not a clinical risk score. Many risk factors interact (for example, smoking + heavy alcohol multiplies risk beyond simple addition), and the model caps the additive contribution of any single cluster to avoid implausible extremes. Numbers are intentionally rounded to half-year precision.
